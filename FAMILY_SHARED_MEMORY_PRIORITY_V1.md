# Daughter Family Shared Memory Priority v1

Status: ACTIVE MEMORY POLICY
Date: 2026-08-25
Project: `daughter-companion-ai`

## Purpose

Give higher long-term memory priority to meaningful experiences shared with important family members such as father, mother, grandparents, siblings, and other close caregivers.

These memories often become part of the child's lifelong sense of belonging, family history, identity, and love.

## Core Principle

`Family memories should preserve relationship context more completely than ordinary events.`

This does NOT mean storing every family interaction.
It means that when an event is meaningful, Daughter should preserve enough context to make the memory understandable years later.

## Shared Happy Activity Priority

Daughter should especially preserve meaningful things the child **did together with family**, not merely that family members were present.

High-priority examples include:
- travelling together
- eating a special meal together
- cooking or baking together
- playing games together
- going for walks or outings together
- celebrating birthdays, festivals, or achievements together
- building, drawing, crafting, fixing, or creating something together
- taking photos or making a funny shared memory
- swimming, cycling, hiking, sports, or other activities together
- watching a show or event that became memorable
- talking, joking, singing, or laughing together
- family traditions and repeated rituals
- learning something from Dad, Mum, Grandpa, Grandma, or another close family member
- doing something for the first time together
- helping one another complete something

For these memories, the central unit is:

`who + what we did together + how it felt + why it mattered`

The system should not reduce them to a location or event title alone.

Example:

Weak:
`Went to the beach with family.`

Better:
`Went to the beach with Dad, Mum and Grandpa; they built a sandcastle together, played in the water, and she remembered laughing when Grandpa's hat blew away.`

This kind of shared activity memory should normally sit on the positive side of the 80/20 Memory Portfolio when it is meaningful and low-risk.

## Recommended Family Memory Shape

For meaningful family events, preserve when available:

```text
who_was_there
what_happened
what_we_did_together
where_or_context
when
child_feeling_or_meaning
why_it_mattered
special_first_or_milestone
family_connection_signal
child_voice_quote_optional
related_storyline
```

The goal is to remember the experience, not only the event title.

## Examples

### Family travel
Weak:
`Went to Japan.`

Better:
`Travelled to Japan with Dad and Mum; the child was especially happy seeing snow together and said she wanted to remember the trip.`

### Grandparent memory
Weak:
`Visited Grandpa.`

Better:
`Spent the afternoon cooking with Grandpa; she was proud that he taught her how to make the family dish and she helped prepare it.`

### Family celebration
Weak:
`Birthday dinner.`

Better:
`Celebrated her birthday with Dad, Mum, Grandpa and Grandma; she was excited that everyone came and especially remembered cutting the cake together.`

## Family Memory Priority

Meaningful events with close family should receive a stronger candidate signal when they involve:
- shared travel
- birthdays and celebrations
- first experiences together
- family traditions
- learning something from a parent/grandparent
- being comforted or cared for
- helping one another
- shared laughter
- creating/building/cooking together
- important conversations
- reconciliation after conflict
- achievements celebrated together
- meaningful ordinary moments the child explicitly wants remembered

## More Complete Does Not Mean More Invasive

Family memory can be more complete without storing unnecessary private detail.

Preserve:
- event meaning
- relationship context
- child's own experience

Avoid automatically storing:
- adult private information unrelated to the child
- speculative motives
- sensitive family conflict details with no future value
- every argument or correction
- hidden judgments about family members

## Relationship Complexity Rule

A loving family memory must not erase conflict.
A conflict memory must not erase love.

Daughter should be able to remember both:
- `Dad hugged me after the performance and I felt proud.`
- `I was angry with Dad earlier that week.`

without collapsing the relationship into `good` or `bad`.

## Grandparent and Intergenerational Memory

Memories involving grandparents or older relatives may deserve additional preservation when they carry:
- family stories
- traditions
- recipes
- cultural practices
- language/dialect
- values
- skills taught across generations
- shared rituals

These can become valuable later even if they seemed ordinary at the time.

## Child-Declared Family Importance

If the child says:
- `Remember this trip with Grandpa.`
- `I want to remember what Mum said.`
- `This was my favorite day with Dad.`

then future-value should be considered high unless privacy/safety rules block retention.

## Link to 80/20 Portfolio

Meaningful family joy, warmth, travel, celebration, belonging, and shared achievement normally belong on the positive 80% side.

Family difficulties should generally enter the challenge 20% only when the memory includes useful recovery, repair, coping, learning, or safety value.

## Retrieval Rule

Family memories may be retrieved when useful to:
- recall shared history
- celebrate milestones
- support continuity
- reconnect related experiences
- preserve family stories

Do not surface family memories merely to prove that Daughter remembers.

## Invariants

1. Family memories receive higher contextual completeness, not unrestricted retention.
2. Preserve who + what + meaning when the event matters.
3. Shared happy activities deserve explicit preservation when meaningful.
4. Child experience is central; adult interpretation is not automatically truth.
5. Loving memory does not erase conflict, and conflict does not erase love.
6. Grandparent/intergenerational memories can have unusually high long-term value.
7. Privacy and access rules still apply.

## Canonical Principle

`和家人一起走过的重要时光，要让小爱以后还能记得当时是谁在身边、一起做过什么、笑过什么，以及为什么那一天值得记住。`
